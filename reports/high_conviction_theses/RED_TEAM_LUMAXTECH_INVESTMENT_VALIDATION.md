## 6. Downside & Margin of Safety (Multiplicative Formulation)

$$\text{Price CAGR} = (1 + \text{EPS CAGR}) \times \left(\frac{P/E_{\text{term}}}{P/E_{\text{entry}}}\right)^{1/N} - 1$$
$$\text{Total Shareholder Return (TSR CAGR)} \approx \text{Price CAGR} + \text{Dividend Yield}$$

### Stress Test Scenario:
- **Operational Deceleration**: Owner's EPS CAGR slows to **$10.0\%$** (well below Bear case due to automotive destocking).
- **Valuation Derating**: P/E compresses from **$19.9\times$ down to $13.5\times$** (a severe $-32.2\%$ multiple derating to trough cyclical levels).

### Exact Output:
1. **Terminal EPS**: $26.08 \times (1.10)^3 = \mathbf{₹34.71}$.
2. **Terminal Price**: $34.71 \times 13.5 = \mathbf{₹468.60}$.
3. **Price CAGR**:
   $$\text{Price CAGR} = 1.10 \times \left(\frac{13.5}{19.9}\right)^{1/3} - 1 = 1.10 \times 0.8787 - 1 = \mathbf{-3.34\% \text{ per annum}}$$
4. **Total Shareholder Return (with 1.8% annual dividend yield)**:
   $$\text{TSR CAGR} \approx -3.34\% + 1.80\% = \mathbf{-1.54\% \text{ per annum} \quad (-4.5\% \text{ cumulative 3-year return})}$$

> **Hostile Downside Verdict**: **Capital preservation and strong modeled downside resilience under the specified terminal scenario.** Even under a simultaneous severe 32% multiple contraction (to 13.5x P/E) and growth deceleration to 10%, the 3-year modeled downside is constrained to $-1.5\%$ per annum.
